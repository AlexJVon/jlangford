Plan Licensing

Already-designed plans from the Plan Library can be licensed to build as drawn or adapted to your lot — a faster, more affordable start than designing from scratch.

How licensing works
  • A license grants the right to build the plan once at one address; the drawings remain the architect's instruments of service
  • Modifications: mirror, resize, re-style elevations, add or remove rooms, adapt foundation for site
  • Every build requires site-specific engineering, energy compliance and local permitting
  • Builders: ask about multi-build licensing for neighborhoods

The process

From first meeting to move-in

Most projects move through these phases. Smaller projects or licensed plans may combine or skip some of them.

01

Consultation & site visit

We talk about how you live, what you need, your budget and timeline, and walk the property. You'll leave with a clear sense of what's possible.

Deliverables
  • Program (list of rooms and sizes)
  • Zoning, setback, flood-zone and deed-restriction review
  • Survey / tree survey requirements
02

Schematic design

The first sketches: where the house sits on the lot, how rooms connect, and the overall shape and character. This is the time for big changes.

Deliverables
  • Site plan and floor-plan options
  • Preliminary elevations and massing
  • Rough area take-offs for early budgeting
03

Design development

The chosen design gets real: exact dimensions, windows, materials, the structural approach and systems. You'll know what you're building.

Deliverables
  • Dimensioned plans, elevations and building sections
  • Window/door selections; exterior material palette
  • Structural and MEP coordination begins
04

Construction documents

The detailed drawing set your builder prices and builds from, and the building department reviews for your permit.

Deliverables
  • Permit-ready set (see sheet list)
  • Wall sections, details and schedules
  • Engineer-sealed structural sheets; energy compliance
05

Permitting & bidding

Drawings go to the building department, and to one or more builders for pricing. Questions from either get answered quickly.

Deliverables
  • Responses to plan-review comments
  • Bid clarifications and addenda
  • Help comparing bids line-by-line
06

Construction administration

While it's being built, the architect is the builder's go-to for questions and your advocate for getting what was designed.

Deliverables
  • RFI responses and clarification sketches
  • Review of submittals and shop drawings (trusses, windows, cabinetry)
  • Site visits and punch-list review

Common questions

Questions people ask

Do I really need an architect?

For many houses in Florida a licensed architect isn't legally required — but the design decisions made before construction affect cost, comfort and resale far more than anything decided on site. An architect helps you avoid expensive changes during construction, gets the most from your lot, and gives your builder clear drawings to price accurately. Commercial and mixed-use buildings generally do require an architect.

How are fees set?

Fees depend on the size and complexity of the project and how much of the process you need. Common arrangements are a fixed fee, an hourly rate, or a percentage of construction cost. Licensing an existing plan from the library is typically the most economical option. Get in touch for a proposal.

Can I buy one of the plans in the library?

Yes — most plans can be licensed to build. They can be built as drawn or modified for your lot, budget and needs. Every build still needs site-specific engineering, energy compliance and a local permit. See Plan Licensing.

Can you change a plan to fit my lot?

Usually. Common changes include mirroring the plan, adjusting width or depth, moving the garage to the alley, adding a bedroom or porch, changing the exterior style, or adapting the foundation for a flood zone or sloped site.

Who owns the drawings?

Architectural drawings are the architect's instruments of service and are protected by copyright. The client receives a license to use them to build the project. Re-using drawings for another house requires a new license.

Where do you work?

The studio is in Gainesville, Florida, and is licensed in Florida, Georgia and South Carolina, with projects across the Southeastern United States.

How long does design take?

It varies with the size of the project and how quickly decisions are made. Permitting time depends on the local building department. A timeline is part of every proposal.

I'm a builder — can I get a plan family for a neighborhood?

Yes. The studio has designed coordinated plan families and pattern books for neighborhoods like Laney Walker/Bethlehem in Augusta, Georgia. Ask about multi-lot licensing.
